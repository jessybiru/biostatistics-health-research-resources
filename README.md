# 📚 Biostatistics & Health Research Resources

### Free learning resources for biostatistics, epidemiology, statistical programming and health research

Welcome to this repository!!

This is a curated collection of freely accessible textbooks, online courses, statistical tutorials, practical exercises, datasets, code examples and teaching materials.

As a biostatistician and researcher, I regularly come across valuable learning resources that can help students, researchers and professionals strengthen their statistical and analytical skills.

I have been sharing many of these resources on LinkedIn and created this repository to bring them together in one place, making them easier to find and revisit.

---

##  Who is this repository for?

- Postgraduate students studying biostatistics, epidemiology and public health
- Researchers working with health and population data
- Biostatisticians, statisticians and data analysts
- Lecturers looking for practical teaching materials
- Beginners learning statistical programming
- Professionals interested in applied statistical methods

## 📉 Longitudinal & Multilevel Analysis


### 1. Analyzing Longitudinal Data Using Multilevel Modeling

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the seminar](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/other/mult-pkg/seminars/mlm-longitudinal/)

A practical introduction to multilevel approaches for analysing repeated observations over time.

**Includes:**
- Repeated measures ANOVA versus mixed models
- Longitudinal growth models
- Random intercepts and slopes
- Changes in outcomes over time
- Modelling treatment effects over time
- Examples using HLM, SAS and other software

**Best for:** Researchers analysing cohort studies, repeated measurements and longitudinal intervention data.

### 2. An Introduction to Linear Mixed-Effects Modeling in R

**Authors:** Violet A. Brown and colleagues  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Read the open-access tutorial](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245920960351)  
**Code and data:** [Access the OSF materials](https://osf.io/v6qag/)

A published tutorial introducing the theory and practical implementation of linear mixed-effects models using R.

**Includes:**
- Fixed and random effects
- Model specification
- Fitting mixed-effects models in R
- Interpretation of model output
- Worked examples
- Downloadable data and R scripts

**Best for:** Researchers with basic regression knowledge who are new to mixed-effects modelling.

### 3. CRAN Task View: Mixed, Multilevel and Hierarchical Models

**Provider:** Comprehensive R Archive Network (CRAN)  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Explore the task view](https://cran.r-project.org/web/views/MixedModels.html)

A reference guide to R packages for fitting multilevel, hierarchical and mixed-effects models.

**Includes:**
- Linear mixed-effects models
- Generalised linear mixed models
- Nested and crossed random effects
- Frequentist and Bayesian approaches
- Links to packages such as lme4, nlme and glmmTMB

**Best for:** R users selecting appropriate software packages for hierarchical or repeated-measures data.

### 4. Multilevel and Longitudinal Modelling Using Stata

**Provider:** StataCorp  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Watch the free webinar](https://www.stata.com/training/webinar_series/multilevel-mixed-effects-models/)

A recorded webinar demonstrating how to analyse nested and longitudinal data using Stata.

**Includes:**
- Random-intercept and random-slope models
- Linear mixed-effects models
- The mixed command
- Predictions and visualisation
- Introduction to models for categorical and count outcomes
- Downloadable webinar materials

**Best for:** Researchers working with clustered, hierarchical or longitudinal data in Stata.

### 5. EasyLME: Longitudinal Data Analysis

**Authors:** Jessica Murphy, Nicholas Weaver and Audrey Hendricks  
**Institution:** University of Colorado Denver  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Open the EasyLME application](https://shiny.clas.ucdenver.edu/EasyLME/)

An interactive web application for analysing longitudinal data using linear mixed-effects models in R.

**Includes:**
- Descriptive summaries
- Longitudinal exploratory plots
- Mixed-effects model results
- Likelihood-ratio tests for nested models
- Model diagnostic plots
- Fitted trajectories

**Best for:** Researchers exploring longitudinal mixed-effects models through an interactive interface.

**Note:** The application assumes continuous outcome and time variables, so it is not suitable for every longitudinal analysis.

**Methodological note:** Longitudinal data and multilevel data are related but not identical. Model selection should consider the study design, outcome distribution, clustering structure, within-subject correlation, missing-data assumptions and research estimand.

## 🌍 Epidemiology


### 1. Principles of Epidemiology in Public Health Practice (3rd Edition)

**Provider:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the free self-study textbook](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/6914)

A foundational introduction to applied epidemiology and biostatistics for public health professionals.

**Includes:**
- Introduction to epidemiology
- Summarising epidemiological data
- Measures of disease frequency and risk
- Tables, graphs and public health data presentation
- Public health surveillance
- Outbreak investigation
- Six structured lessons with exercises

**Best for:** Students, field epidemiologists, public health practitioners and researchers beginning epidemiological training.

**Note:** This is an established but older edition; consult current public health guidance when applying surveillance or outbreak response procedures.

### 2. Foundations of Epidemiology

**Author:** Marit Bovbjerg  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the open-access textbook](https://upliftlab.org/epitext/)

An introductory epidemiology textbook designed for public health students and practitioners.

**Includes:**
- Incidence and prevalence
- Epidemiological study designs
- Measures of association
- Random error and statistical inference
- Bias and confounding
- Effect modification
- Causal thinking
- Screening

**Best for:** Undergraduate and postgraduate students who need a structured introduction or refresher in epidemiological methods.

### 3. The Epidemiologist R Handbook

**Provider:** Applied Epi and contributing epidemiologists  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the handbook](https://www.epirhandbook.com/en/)

A practical reference for conducting epidemiological and public health analyses using R.

**Includes:**
- Data cleaning and management
- Descriptive epidemiology
- Epidemiological tables and visualisation
- Outbreak investigation examples
- Reproducible R code
- Example datasets
- Offline-access options

**Best for:** Field epidemiologists, surveillance officers, public health researchers and analysts using R.

**Note:** This resource is also listed under R Programming because it is relevant to both categories.

### 4. Causal Inference: What If

**Authors:** Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the free textbook](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook)

A comprehensive textbook on estimating causal effects from observational and experimental data.

**Includes:**
- Counterfactuals and causal effects
- Randomised and observational studies
- Confounding and selection bias
- Causal diagrams
- Standardisation and inverse probability weighting
- The parametric g-formula
- Marginal structural models
- Time-varying treatments and confounders
- Downloadable datasets and supporting code

**Best for:** Epidemiologists, biostatisticians and postgraduate researchers interested in causal inference and advanced observational study methods.

### 5. Causal Inference: Practical R and Stata Code

**Book authors:** Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins  
**Code contributors:** Joy Shi, Sean McGrath, Eleanor Murray, Roger Logan and Tom Palmer  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the practical code and exercises](https://remlapmot.github.io/cibookex-r/)

A practical companion resource containing reproducible examples associated with the textbook *Causal Inference: What If*.

**Includes:**
- R and Stata code
- Worked examples
- Causal inference methods
- Reproducible analyses
- Downloadable code materials

**Best for:** Researchers who want to implement causal inference methods using R or Stata.

**Note:** Use this resource alongside the original textbook for the methodological explanations.


## 🔬 Research Methods


### 1. Sampling Theory and Methods (STAT 506)

**Provider:** Pennsylvania State University  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free course notes](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat506/)

A graduate-level introduction to probability sampling and estimation.

**Includes:**
- Simple random sampling
- Stratified and cluster sampling
- Systematic and multistage sampling
- Sample size estimation
- Unequal probability sampling
- Survey estimation and confidence intervals
- Worked examples and exercises

**Best for:** Postgraduate students and researchers designing population-based surveys.

### 2. OpenEpi: Epidemiological Statistics and Sample Size

**Developers:** Andrew G. Dean, Kevin M. Sullivan and Minn M. Soe  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access OpenEpi](https://www.openepi.com/)

A free collection of epidemiological statistical calculators.

**Includes:**
- Sample size for estimating proportions
- Sample size for cohort and case-control studies
- Sample size for clinical trials
- Confidence intervals
- Two-by-two tables
- Measures of association
- Statistical tests

**Best for:** Researchers planning epidemiological studies or performing basic statistical calculations.

**Note:** Sample size calculations must match the primary research objective, study design, analysis plan and underlying assumptions.

### 3. EQUATOR Network: Research Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** EQUATOR Network  
**Level:** All levels  
**Resource:** [Explore reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/)

An international resource supporting transparent and accurate reporting of health research.

**Includes:**
- STROBE for observational studies
- CONSORT for randomised trials
- PRISMA for systematic reviews
- STARD for diagnostic accuracy studies
- TRIPOD+AI for prediction models
- SRQR for qualitative research
- Reporting checklists and explanatory materials

**Best for:** Researchers preparing manuscripts, theses and research reports.

### 4. PRISMA 2020: Systematic Review Reporting

**Provider:** PRISMA Statement  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/)

An evidence-based reporting guideline for systematic reviews.

**Includes:**
- PRISMA 2020 checklist
- PRISMA flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration guidance
- Reporting guidance for review methods and results

**Best for:** Researchers writing systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

**Note:** PRISMA primarily guides reporting. It does not replace methodological guidance on how to conduct a systematic review.

### 5. Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions

**Provider:** Cochrane  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the online handbook](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook)

A comprehensive methodological reference for planning, conducting and interpreting systematic reviews of interventions.

**Includes:**
- Formulating review questions
- Developing eligibility criteria
- Literature searching
- Study selection and data extraction
- Risk-of-bias assessment
- Meta-analysis
- Heterogeneity and sensitivity analysis
- Interpretation and presentation of findings

**Best for:** Researchers conducting systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

### 6. CONSORT and SPIRIT Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** CONSORT–SPIRIT initiative  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the guidelines](https://www.consort-spirit.org/)

International guidance for transparent reporting of randomised controlled trials and clinical trial protocols.

**Includes:**
- CONSORT 2025 reporting checklist
- SPIRIT 2025 protocol checklist
- Trial flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration documents
- Guidance on trial methods and reporting

**Best for:** Clinical researchers developing trial protocols or preparing randomised trial manuscripts.

### 7. Quarto: Reproducible Research and Scientific Reporting

**Provider:** Posit and Quarto contributors  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the Quarto documentation](https://quarto.org/docs/guide/)

An open-source publishing system for producing reproducible scientific documents.

**Includes:**
- Reports using R, Python and Julia
- Integration with Jupyter notebooks
- Statistical tables and figures
- PDF, Word and HTML output
- Citations and bibliographies
- Presentations, websites and books

**Best for:** Researchers, biostatisticians and lecturers preparing reproducible reports, teaching materials and manuscripts.
