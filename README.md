# 📚 Biostatistics & Health Research Resources

### Free learning resources for biostatistics, epidemiology, statistical programming and health research

Welcome to this repository!!

This is a curated collection of freely accessible textbooks, online courses, statistical tutorials, practical exercises, datasets, code examples and teaching materials.

As a biostatistician and researcher, I regularly come across valuable learning resources that can help students, researchers and professionals strengthen their statistical and analytical skills.

I have been sharing many of these resources on LinkedIn and created this repository to bring them together in one place, making them easier to find and revisit.

---

##  Who is this repository for?

- Postgraduate students studying biostatistics, epidemiology and public health
- Researchers working with health and population data
- Biostatisticians, statisticians and data analysts
- Lecturers looking for practical teaching materials
- Beginners learning statistical programming
- Professionals interested in applied statistical methods

## 🔬 Research Methods


### 1. Sampling Theory and Methods (STAT 506)

**Provider:** Pennsylvania State University  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free course notes](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat506/)

A graduate-level introduction to probability sampling and estimation.

**Includes:**
- Simple random sampling
- Stratified and cluster sampling
- Systematic and multistage sampling
- Sample size estimation
- Unequal probability sampling
- Survey estimation and confidence intervals
- Worked examples and exercises

**Best for:** Postgraduate students and researchers designing population-based surveys.

### 2. OpenEpi: Epidemiological Statistics and Sample Size

**Developers:** Andrew G. Dean, Kevin M. Sullivan and Minn M. Soe  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access OpenEpi](https://www.openepi.com/)

A free collection of epidemiological statistical calculators.

**Includes:**
- Sample size for estimating proportions
- Sample size for cohort and case-control studies
- Sample size for clinical trials
- Confidence intervals
- Two-by-two tables
- Measures of association
- Statistical tests

**Best for:** Researchers planning epidemiological studies or performing basic statistical calculations.

**Note:** Sample size calculations must match the primary research objective, study design, analysis plan and underlying assumptions.

### 3. EQUATOR Network: Research Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** EQUATOR Network  
**Level:** All levels  
**Resource:** [Explore reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/)

An international resource supporting transparent and accurate reporting of health research.

**Includes:**
- STROBE for observational studies
- CONSORT for randomised trials
- PRISMA for systematic reviews
- STARD for diagnostic accuracy studies
- TRIPOD+AI for prediction models
- SRQR for qualitative research
- Reporting checklists and explanatory materials

**Best for:** Researchers preparing manuscripts, theses and research reports.

### 4. PRISMA 2020: Systematic Review Reporting

**Provider:** PRISMA Statement  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/)

An evidence-based reporting guideline for systematic reviews.

**Includes:**
- PRISMA 2020 checklist
- PRISMA flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration guidance
- Reporting guidance for review methods and results

**Best for:** Researchers writing systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

**Note:** PRISMA primarily guides reporting. It does not replace methodological guidance on how to conduct a systematic review.

### 5. Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions

**Provider:** Cochrane  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the online handbook](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook)

A comprehensive methodological reference for planning, conducting and interpreting systematic reviews of interventions.

**Includes:**
- Formulating review questions
- Developing eligibility criteria
- Literature searching
- Study selection and data extraction
- Risk-of-bias assessment
- Meta-analysis
- Heterogeneity and sensitivity analysis
- Interpretation and presentation of findings

**Best for:** Researchers conducting systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

### 6. CONSORT and SPIRIT Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** CONSORT–SPIRIT initiative  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the guidelines](https://www.consort-spirit.org/)

International guidance for transparent reporting of randomised controlled trials and clinical trial protocols.

**Includes:**
- CONSORT 2025 reporting checklist
- SPIRIT 2025 protocol checklist
- Trial flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration documents
- Guidance on trial methods and reporting

**Best for:** Clinical researchers developing trial protocols or preparing randomised trial manuscripts.

### 7. Quarto: Reproducible Research and Scientific Reporting

**Provider:** Posit and Quarto contributors  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the Quarto documentation](https://quarto.org/docs/guide/)

An open-source publishing system for producing reproducible scientific documents.

**Includes:**
- Reports using R, Python and Julia
- Integration with Jupyter notebooks
- Statistical tables and figures
- PDF, Word and HTML output
- Citations and bibliographies
- Presentations, websites and books

**Best for:** Researchers, biostatisticians and lecturers preparing reproducible reports, teaching materials and manuscripts.


## 🎓 Teaching & Practical Resources


### 1. Statistical Practice in Epidemiology using R (SPE-R)

**Provider:** SPE-R course team  
**Software:** R  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the course materials](https://spe-r.github.io/)  
**Source code:** [GitHub repository](https://github.com/SPE-R/SPE)

A comprehensive practical course for learning and teaching statistical methods in epidemiology.

**Includes:**
- Lecture slides
- Practical exercises booklet
- Separate booklet with worked solutions
- Downloadable datasets
- R scripts and functions
- Course setup instructions
- Downloadable course materials

**Best for:** Epidemiology lecturers, postgraduate students and researchers looking for structured statistical practicals.

### 2. Data Carpentry: Social Sciences

**Provider:** The Carpentries  
**Software:** R, spreadsheets and OpenRefine  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the lessons](https://www.datacarpentry.org/lessons/)

A collection of reusable teaching materials introducing researchers to data organisation, cleaning and analysis.

**Includes:**
- Spreadsheet data organisation
- Data cleaning using OpenRefine
- Data analysis and visualisation using R
- Practical exercises
- Example datasets
- Instructor notes and teaching guidance

**Best for:** Lecturers and researchers teaching introductory data management and statistical programming.

**Note:** The Social Sciences curriculum uses survey data from farming households in eastern Africa.

### 3. OpenIntro Statistics

**Authors:** David M. Diez, Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel and Christopher D. Barr  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free textbook](https://www.openintro.org/book/os/)

An introductory statistics textbook designed for classroom teaching and independent learning.

**Includes:**
- Free textbook PDF
- Statistical exercises
- Learning objectives
- Worked examples
- Companion datasets
- Supporting teaching resources

**Best for:** Undergraduate students, postgraduate beginners and lecturers teaching introductory statistics.

### 4. OpenIntro Statistical Datasets

**Provider:** OpenIntro  
**Software:** R, Stata, SAS, Python and spreadsheets  
**Level:** All levels  
**Resource:** [Browse the datasets](https://www.openintro.us/data/)

A collection of datasets suitable for classroom demonstrations, statistical exercises and independent learning.

**Includes:**
- Downloadable CSV datasets
- Tab-delimited files
- R data files
- Datasets covering health, education, social and economic topics
- Data used in OpenIntro textbooks

**Best for:** Lecturers looking for accessible datasets for teaching statistical methods.

### 5. UCLA Statistical Consulting Resources

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Software:** R, Stata, SAS and other statistical packages  
**Level:** Beginner to advanced  
**Resource:** [Explore the learning resources](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/)

A comprehensive collection of statistical tutorials and practical examples.

**Includes:**
- Statistical learning modules
- Annotated statistical output
- Data analysis examples
- Regression tutorials
- Survival analysis workshops
- Missing-data tutorials
- Multilevel modelling examples
- Example code and datasets

**Best for:** Lecturers, postgraduate students and researchers looking for reproducible examples of statistical methods.

### 6. The Carpentries: Programming and Data Skills Lessons

**Provider:** The Carpentries  
**Software:** R, Python, spreadsheets, SQL and other tools  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate

An extensive collection of collaboratively developed lessons for teaching research computing and data skills.

- Programming lessons
- Data cleaning and organisation

- Reproducible research workflows
- Practical exercises

- Links to source repositories

**Best for:** Researchers and lecturers preparing hands-on workshops in statistical programming and research data management.
