# 📚 Biostatistics & Health Research Resources

### Free learning resources for biostatistics, epidemiology, statistical programming and health research

Welcome to this repository!!

This is a curated collection of freely accessible textbooks, online courses, statistical tutorials, practical exercises, datasets, code examples and teaching materials.

As a biostatistician and researcher, I regularly come across valuable learning resources that can help students, researchers and professionals strengthen their statistical and analytical skills.

I have been sharing many of these resources on LinkedIn and created this repository to bring them together in one place, making them easier to find and revisit.

---

##  Who is this repository for?

- Postgraduate students studying biostatistics, epidemiology and public health
- Researchers working with health and population data
- Biostatisticians, statisticians and data analysts
- Lecturers looking for practical teaching materials
- Beginners learning statistical programming
- Professionals interested in applied statistical methods

## 📘 R Programming

### 1. R for Data Science (2nd Edition)

**Authors:** Hadley Wickham, Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel and Garrett Grolemund  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Read the free online book](https://r4ds.hadley.nz/)

A practical introduction to working with data in R, covering data import, cleaning, transformation, visualisation and reproducible reporting.

**Includes:** R code, worked examples, exercises and a companion exercise-solutions website.

### 2. R for Health Data Science

**Authors:** Ewen Harrison and Riinu Pius  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Read the free online book](https://argoshare.is.ed.ac.uk/healthyr_book/)

An applied introduction to R for healthcare professionals and health researchers. Covers data manipulation, visualisation, regression, survival analysis, missing data and reproducible reporting.

**Includes:** Health-related examples, R code and practical demonstrations.

### 3. The Epidemiologist R Handbook

**Authors:** Neale Batra and contributing epidemiologists  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the handbook](https://www.epirhandbook.com/en/)

A task-oriented R reference for epidemiology and public health, with examples covering common epidemiological data management, analysis and reporting tasks.

**Includes:** Example datasets, reproducible R code and an offline-access option.

### 4. Introduction to R for Health Data Analysis

**Authors:** Ehsan Karim, An Hoang and Yang Qu  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Read the online textbook](https://ehsanx.github.io/intro2R/)

An introduction to R programming and data wrangling using the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES).

**Includes:** Health survey examples, R code and practical learning activities.


### 5. Statistical Practice in Epidemiology using R (SPE-R)

**Provider:** SPE-R course team  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the course materials](https://spe-r.github.io/)

A practical course in statistical methods for epidemiological research using R.

**Includes:** Lecture slides, downloadable datasets, R scripts, a practical exercises booklet and a separate booklet with worked solutions.

**Best for:** Epidemiology students, postgraduate researchers and lecturers looking for ready-to-use practical teaching materials.

### 6. Mastering Health Data Science Using R

**Author:** Alice Paul  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Read the online book](https://alicepaul.github.io/health-data-science-using-r/)

An applied R textbook using public health examples to teach data management, visualisation, hypothesis testing, regression and programming.

**Includes:** Case studies involving tuberculosis screening, COVID-19, blood lead levels and hypertension, tuberculosis risk prediction and simulation studies. Also provides exercises, Quarto materials and downloadable source files.

**Best for:** Public health students and health researchers interested in applying R to real research questions.

### 7. Modern Statistics with R (2nd Edition)

**Author:** Måns Thulin  
**Level:** Beginner to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the free online book](https://modernstatisticswithr.com/)

A comprehensive introduction to modern statistical analysis using R, from data wrangling and exploratory analysis to statistical inference, predictive modelling and simulation.

**Includes:** More than 200 exercises with worked solutions, example datasets and R code.

**Best for:** Students, researchers and lecturers seeking a structured statistics textbook with practical exercises.

### 8. Applied Epi: Free R Tutorials

**Provider:** Applied Epi  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the tutorials](https://appliedepi.org/resources/tutorials.html)

Self-paced R tutorials developed around realistic public health data challenges.

**Includes:** Interactive exercises covering R fundamentals, importing data, cleaning datasets and other practical epidemiological tasks. Available in multiple languages.

**Access:** Free account registration is required.

**Best for:** Beginners learning R for epidemiology, surveillance and public health practice.

### 9. Applied Epi: Epidemiology Case Studies

**Provider:** Applied Epi and collaborating organisations  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Explore the case study repository](https://casestudies.appliedepi.org/)

A collection of practical case studies designed to help learners apply R to epidemiological investigations and public health data analysis.

**Includes:** Realistic scenarios, tasks, questions and generated or anonymised datasets. Topics include outbreak investigations and surveillance data analysis.

**Best for:** Epidemiologists, public health practitioners and researchers who want additional hands-on experience.

### 10. swirl: Learn R, in R

**Provider:** swirl project  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Visit swirl](https://swirlstats.com/)

An interactive learning package that teaches R programming directly inside the R console.

**Includes:** Guided lessons and exercises with immediate feedback.

**Getting started:**

```r
install.packages("swirl")
library(swirl)
swirl()
```

**Best for:** Beginners who prefer learning R by running commands rather than only reading tutorials.

### 11. An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in R (2nd Edition)

**Authors:** Gareth James, Daniela Witten, Trevor Hastie and Robert Tibshirani  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free textbook](https://www.statlearning.com/)

A widely used introduction to statistical learning, covering regression, classification, resampling, regularisation, tree-based methods, survival analysis and unsupervised learning.

**Includes:** Free textbook PDF, R practical labs and companion video lectures.

**Best for:** Researchers and students interested in statistical learning and predictive modelling.

### 12. Practical Statistics in Medicine with R

**Author:** Konstantinos I. Bougioukas  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the online survival analysis chapter](https://practical-stats-med-r.netlify.app/survival)

A practical medical statistics resource demonstrating statistical concepts and methods using R and healthcare examples.

**Includes:** R code and worked explanations of censoring, survival probabilities, Kaplan–Meier estimation, median survival and follow-up, log-rank tests, hazard ratios and Cox proportional hazards regression.

**Best for:** Medical researchers and postgraduate students learning survival analysis.

**Note:** The linked online chapter is accessible for reading; the commercially published textbook is not necessarily available as a free PDF.


## 📗 Stata


### 1. Stata Learning Modules

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the learning modules](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/modules/)

A collection of tutorials covering Stata fundamentals, data management, statistical tests, graphics and working with datasets.

**Includes:** Command examples, explanations and demonstrations of common Stata workflows.

**Best for:** Students and researchers learning Stata or strengthening their data management skills.

### 2. Stata Annotated Output

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to advanced  
**Resource:** [Explore annotated output](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/output/stata-annotated-output/)

A collection of worked statistical examples explaining how to interpret Stata output.

**Includes:** Examples covering linear and logistic regression, Poisson and negative binomial regression, ordinal and multinomial models, factor analysis and other statistical procedures.

**Best for:** Researchers who want to understand and correctly interpret statistical results.

### 3. Survival Analysis with Stata

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the survival analysis seminar](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/seminars/stata-survival/)

An applied introduction to time-to-event analysis using data from a substance-use treatment study.

**Includes:** Kaplan–Meier curves, log-rank tests, Cox proportional hazards regression, assessment of proportional hazards, model diagnostics and Stata commands.

**Best for:** Clinical researchers, epidemiologists and postgraduate students analysing time-to-event outcomes.

### 4. Survey Data Analysis in Stata

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the survey analysis seminar](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/seminars/survey-data-analysis-with-stata-15/)

A practical guide to analysing complex survey data using Stata, with examples based on the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES).

**Includes:** Survey weights, stratification, clustering, survey design specification, descriptive analysis and regression examples.

**Best for:** Researchers working with DHS, NHANES and other complex survey datasets.

### 5. Multiple Imputation in Stata

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the multiple imputation workshop](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/seminars/multiple-imputation-in-stata-new/)

A workshop on handling missing data using multiple imputation in Stata.

**Includes:** Workshop slides, downloadable Stata code and explanations of missing-data mechanisms and multiple imputation procedures.

**Best for:** Postgraduate students and researchers dealing with incomplete datasets.

### 6. Stata Textbook Examples

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Explore textbook examples](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/stata/examples/)

A collection of Stata examples illustrating statistical methods from published textbooks.

**Includes:** Replicable analyses and downloadable datasets covering regression, longitudinal analysis, multilevel modelling, survival analysis and other statistical methods.

**Best for:** Students, lecturers and researchers who want to reproduce textbook analyses using Stata.

**Note:** The examples and datasets are accessible online; the associated textbooks are not necessarily free.

### 7. Stata Basics

**Provider:** StataCorp  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access Stata Basics](https://www.stata.com/links/stata-basics/)

Official introductory learning materials demonstrating how to perform common tasks in Stata.

**Includes:** Worked examples, command syntax, explanations, links to video demonstrations and relevant Stata manuals.

**Best for:** Beginners learning Stata and experienced users reviewing specific commands.


## 📙 SAS


### 1. SAS OnDemand for Academics

**Provider:** SAS Institute  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access SAS OnDemand for Academics](https://www.sas.com/en_us/software/on-demand-for-academics.html)

A free, cloud-based SAS learning environment that allows students, educators and independent learners to practise SAS programming and statistical analysis without installing SAS locally.

**Includes:** SAS Studio, introductory tutorials and access to the free SAS Programming 1 and Statistics 1 courses.

**Best for:** Beginners who want to learn SAS but do not have access to licensed SAS software.

**Note:** Free access is intended for learning and teaching purposes.

### 2. SAS Programming for R Users

**Provider:** SAS Institute / SAS Communities  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Course materials:** [GitHub repository](https://github.com/sascommunities/sas-prog-for-r-users)  
**Online course:** [SAS Programming for R Users](https://learn.sas.com/course/view.php?id=295)

A comprehensive course designed for experienced R users who want to apply their existing programming and statistical analysis skills in SAS.

**Includes:**
- More than 600 pages of course notes
- Over 80 SAS programs
- Sample datasets in CSV, Excel, text and SAS formats
- Course demonstrations and exercises
- Examples of regression, logistic regression, mixed models, simulation and data manipulation

**Best for:** Researchers, statisticians and data analysts transitioning from R to SAS.

**Note:** Some older setup instructions refer to SAS University Edition, which has been discontinued. Learners can explore SAS OnDemand for Academics instead.

### 3. Introduction to Survival Analysis in SAS

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the survival analysis seminar](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/sas/seminars/sas-survival/)

A practical introduction to survival analysis using data from 500 participants in the Worcester Heart Attack Study.

**Includes:**
- Downloadable study dataset
- Kaplan–Meier survival estimation
- PROC LIFETEST
- Cox proportional hazards regression using PROC PHREG
- Model diagnostics and proportional hazards assessment
- Time-varying covariates

**Best for:** Clinical researchers, epidemiologists and postgraduate students analysing time-to-event outcomes.

### 4. Logistic Regression in SAS

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the logistic regression seminar](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/sas/seminars/logistic-regression-in-sas/)

A practical seminar demonstrating how to fit, interpret and assess logistic regression models using SAS.

**Includes:** SAS code, example data, model interpretation, goodness-of-fit assessment, nonlinear relationships and interaction effects.

**Best for:** Health researchers and postgraduate students analysing binary outcomes.

### 5. Applied Survey Data Analysis Using SAS

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the survey analysis workshop](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/sas/seminars/sas-survey/)

A practical workshop on analysing data collected using complex survey sampling designs.

**Includes:**
- Downloadable presentation slides
- SAS code and example datasets
- Survey weights, stratification and clustering
- PROC SURVEYMEANS and PROC SURVEYFREQ
- PROC SURVEYREG and PROC SURVEYLOGISTIC
- Survey-weighted regression examples

**Best for:** Researchers analysing population-based health surveys, including NHANES and other complex survey datasets.

### 6. UCLA SAS Statistical Workshops

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to advanced  
**Resource:** [Browse the statistical seminar collection](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/other/mult-pkg/seminars/)

A collection of applied SAS workshops covering statistical programming, data analysis and modelling.

**Topics include:**
- Introduction to SAS
- Regression and logistic regression
- Repeated measures analysis
- Multiple imputation
- Survival analysis
- Survey data analysis
- Interactions and SAS macros

**Best for:** Researchers and lecturers looking for method-specific SAS tutorials and teaching examples.

### 7. SAS Annotated Statistical Output

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Explore SAS output examples](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/sas/output/)

Worked statistical examples designed to help learners understand the results generated by SAS procedures.

**Includes:** Statistical output and explanations of coefficients, test statistics, significance tests and model results.

**Best for:** Students and researchers who want to improve their interpretation of statistical analyses.


## 📕 Python


### 1. Python for Data Analysis (3rd Edition)

**Author:** Wes McKinney  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Read the free online book](https://wesmckinney.com/book/)

An open-access online edition of the practical Python data analysis textbook written by the creator of pandas.

**Includes:**
- Python and Jupyter Notebook fundamentals
- Data cleaning and transformation using pandas
- Working with NumPy arrays
- Merging, reshaping and summarising datasets
- Data visualisation and time series analysis
- Code examples and supporting datasets on GitHub

**Best for:** Researchers and analysts transitioning from R, Stata, SAS or Excel to Python.

**Note:** The book is free to read online. The complete book text is not licensed for redistribution.

### 2. Think Stats (3rd Edition)

**Author:** Allen B. Downey  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access Think Stats](https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-stats-3e/)

An applied introduction to probability and statistics using Python, with an emphasis on learning through real data and computational exercises.

**Includes:**
- Executable Jupyter notebooks
- Google Colab compatibility
- Exploratory data analysis
- Probability distributions and statistical inference
- Regression and statistical modelling
- Exercises and code examples

**Best for:** Students and health researchers who want to learn statistical concepts while developing Python programming skills.

### 3. An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in Python

**Authors:** Gareth James, Daniela Witten, Trevor Hastie, Robert Tibshirani and Jonathan Taylor  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Textbook:** [Official website](https://www.statlearning.com/)  
**Practical labs:** [Python labs and datasets](https://intro-stat-learning.github.io/ISLP/labs.html)

A freely accessible statistical learning textbook with practical Python labs demonstrating commonly used predictive modelling methods.

**Includes:**
- Linear and logistic regression
- Cross-validation and bootstrap methods
- Regularisation and variable selection
- Classification and tree-based methods
- Support vector machines and deep learning
- Survival analysis
- Unsupervised learning
- Downloadable lab materials and datasets

**Best for:** Researchers, postgraduate students and analysts interested in statistical learning and predictive modelling.

### 4. statsmodels: Statistical Analysis Examples

**Provider:** statsmodels development team  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Explore the examples](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/index.html)

A collection of practical examples demonstrating statistical analysis and modelling using Python.

**Includes:**
- Linear and logistic regression
- Generalised linear models
- Regression diagnostics
- Mixed-effects models
- Time series analysis
- Statistical tests and model interpretation
- Downloadable Python scripts and Jupyter notebooks

**Best for:** Biostatisticians and researchers who want to perform classical statistical analyses using Python.

### 5. lifelines: Survival Analysis in Python

**Provider:** lifelines project  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the documentation and tutorials](https://lifelines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)

A Python library with practical documentation and examples for analysing time-to-event data.

**Includes:**
- Kaplan–Meier survival estimation
- Cox proportional hazards regression
- Survival curves and model interpretation
- Time-varying covariates
- Proportional hazards assumption checks
- Model diagnostics and prediction

**Best for:** Clinical researchers, epidemiologists and postgraduate students conducting survival analysis.

### 6. PyHealth: Clinical Prediction Tutorials

**Provider:** PyHealth development team  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the tutorials](https://pyhealth.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials.html)

A collection of practical Python tutorials for developing and evaluating machine learning models using healthcare data.

**Includes:**
- Google Colab notebooks
- Public and synthetic healthcare datasets
- In-hospital mortality prediction
- Logistic regression, XGBoost and neural networks
- Model discrimination and calibration
- Confidence intervals and subgroup analysis
- Model interpretation using SHAP
- Patient-level data splitting and leakage prevention

**Best for:** Health researchers interested in clinical prediction modelling and healthcare machine learning.

**Note:** Start with the current PyHealth tutorial series rather than older examples written for previous versions.

### 7. scikit-learn: Practical Machine Learning Examples

**Provider:** scikit-learn development team  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Explore the example gallery](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/index.html)

An extensive collection of worked Python examples demonstrating machine learning methods and model evaluation.

**Includes:**
- Classification and regression
- Cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning
- Feature selection and preprocessing
- Clustering and dimensionality reduction
- Model evaluation and visualisation
- Reproducible Python code

**Best for:** Researchers and analysts learning predictive modelling with Python.

## 📊 Regression & Statistical Modelling

### 1. Regression Methods (STAT 501)

**Provider:** Pennsylvania State University  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free course notes](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat501/)

An applied course covering the principles, assumptions, estimation and interpretation of regression models.

**Includes:**
- Simple and multiple linear regression
- Categorical predictors and interaction effects
- Regression diagnostics and influential observations
- Multicollinearity and data transformations
- Model building and prediction
- Worked examples and learning materials

**Best for:** Postgraduate students, researchers and lecturers teaching regression analysis.

### 2. Analysis of Discrete Data (STAT 504)

**Provider:** Pennsylvania State University  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the free course notes](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat504/)

A graduate-level course on categorical data analysis and generalised linear models, with examples using R and SAS.

**Includes:**
- Contingency tables and measures of association
- Logistic regression
- Poisson and log-linear models
- Multinomial and ordinal logistic regression
- Model diagnostics and goodness-of-fit assessment
- Interpretation of main effects and interactions

**Best for:** Biostatisticians and epidemiologists analysing binary, categorical and count outcomes.

### 3. UCLA Data Analysis Examples

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to advanced  
**Resource:** [Explore data analysis examples](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/other/dae/)

A practical collection of statistical analysis examples organised by method and software.

**Includes:**
- Linear and logistic regression
- Poisson and negative binomial regression
- Ordinal and multinomial logistic regression
- Robust regression
- Mixed-effects models
- Example commands and datasets for multiple statistical packages

**Best for:** Researchers looking for practical examples of statistical models in R, Stata or SAS.

### 4. UCLA Annotated Statistical Output

**Provider:** UCLA Office of Advanced Research Computing  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Explore annotated output](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/other/annotatedoutput/)

A collection of statistical output examples with explanations of how to interpret the results.

**Includes:**
- Regression coefficients and standard errors
- Odds ratios and statistical significance
- Linear, logistic and count regression
- Ordinal and multinomial models
- Software-specific examples

**Best for:** Students and researchers who want to understand and report statistical results correctly.
