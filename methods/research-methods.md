
# Research Methods Resources

A curated collection of freely accessible learning resources covering quantitative research methods, study design, sampling, data collection, research reporting and reproducible scientific research.

These resources include university course materials, methodological guidelines, tutorials, practical examples and tools relevant to biostatistics, epidemiology, public health and health research.

They are intended for undergraduate and postgraduate students, researchers, lecturers and professionals seeking to strengthen their research methodology skills.


### 1. Sampling Theory and Methods (STAT 506)

**Provider:** Pennsylvania State University  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the free course notes](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat506/)

A graduate-level introduction to probability sampling and estimation.

**Includes:**
- Simple random sampling
- Stratified and cluster sampling
- Systematic and multistage sampling
- Sample size estimation
- Unequal probability sampling
- Survey estimation and confidence intervals
- Worked examples and exercises

**Best for:** Postgraduate students and researchers designing population-based surveys.

### 2. OpenEpi: Epidemiological Statistics and Sample Size

**Developers:** Andrew G. Dean, Kevin M. Sullivan and Minn M. Soe  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access OpenEpi](https://www.openepi.com/)

A free collection of epidemiological statistical calculators.

**Includes:**
- Sample size for estimating proportions
- Sample size for cohort and case-control studies
- Sample size for clinical trials
- Confidence intervals
- Two-by-two tables
- Measures of association
- Statistical tests

**Best for:** Researchers planning epidemiological studies or performing basic statistical calculations.

**Note:** Sample size calculations must match the primary research objective, study design, analysis plan and underlying assumptions.

### 3. EQUATOR Network: Research Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** EQUATOR Network  
**Level:** All levels  
**Resource:** [Explore reporting guidelines](https://www.equator-network.org/)

An international resource supporting transparent and accurate reporting of health research.

**Includes:**
- STROBE for observational studies
- CONSORT for randomised trials
- PRISMA for systematic reviews
- STARD for diagnostic accuracy studies
- TRIPOD+AI for prediction models
- SRQR for qualitative research
- Reporting checklists and explanatory materials

**Best for:** Researchers preparing manuscripts, theses and research reports.

### 4. PRISMA 2020: Systematic Review Reporting

**Provider:** PRISMA Statement  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/)

An evidence-based reporting guideline for systematic reviews.

**Includes:**
- PRISMA 2020 checklist
- PRISMA flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration guidance
- Reporting guidance for review methods and results

**Best for:** Researchers writing systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

**Note:** PRISMA primarily guides reporting. It does not replace methodological guidance on how to conduct a systematic review.

### 5. Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions

**Provider:** Cochrane  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the online handbook](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook)

A comprehensive methodological reference for planning, conducting and interpreting systematic reviews of interventions.

**Includes:**
- Formulating review questions
- Developing eligibility criteria
- Literature searching
- Study selection and data extraction
- Risk-of-bias assessment
- Meta-analysis
- Heterogeneity and sensitivity analysis
- Interpretation and presentation of findings

**Best for:** Researchers conducting systematic reviews and meta-analyses.

### 6. CONSORT and SPIRIT Reporting Guidelines

**Provider:** CONSORT–SPIRIT initiative  
**Level:** Intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the guidelines](https://www.consort-spirit.org/)

International guidance for transparent reporting of randomised controlled trials and clinical trial protocols.

**Includes:**
- CONSORT 2025 reporting checklist
- SPIRIT 2025 protocol checklist
- Trial flow diagrams
- Explanation and elaboration documents
- Guidance on trial methods and reporting

**Best for:** Clinical researchers developing trial protocols or preparing randomised trial manuscripts.

### 7. Quarto: Reproducible Research and Scientific Reporting

**Provider:** Posit and Quarto contributors  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the Quarto documentation](https://quarto.org/docs/guide/)

An open-source publishing system for producing reproducible scientific documents.

**Includes:**
- Reports using R, Python and Julia
- Integration with Jupyter notebooks
- Statistical tables and figures
- PDF, Word and HTML output
- Citations and bibliographies
- Presentations, websites and books

**Best for:** Researchers, biostatisticians and lecturers preparing reproducible reports, teaching materials and manuscripts.
