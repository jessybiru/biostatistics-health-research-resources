
# Epidemiology Resources

A curated collection of freely accessible learning resources covering epidemiological principles, study designs, measures of disease frequency and association, epidemiological analysis and public health research.

These resources include online textbooks, university course materials, tutorials, practical exercises and examples using epidemiological and population health data.

They are intended for students, researchers, public health practitioners and professionals seeking to strengthen their epidemiological knowledge and analytical skills.


### 1. Principles of Epidemiology in Public Health Practice (3rd Edition)

**Provider:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the free self-study textbook](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/6914)

A foundational introduction to applied epidemiology and biostatistics for public health professionals.

**Includes:**
- Introduction to epidemiology
- Summarising epidemiological data
- Measures of disease frequency and risk
- Tables, graphs and public health data presentation
- Public health surveillance
- Outbreak investigation
- Six structured lessons with exercises

**Best for:** Students, field epidemiologists, public health practitioners and researchers beginning epidemiological training.

**Note:** This 2006 edition has been superseded by a newer CDC version. It remains useful for foundational epidemiological concepts, but readers should consult updated CDC guidance for current public health practice.

### 2. Foundations of Epidemiology

**Author:** Marit Bovbjerg  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Resource:** [Access the open-access textbook](https://upliftlab.org/epitext/)

An introductory epidemiology textbook designed for public health students and practitioners.

**Includes:**
- Incidence and prevalence
- Epidemiological study designs
- Measures of association
- Random error and statistical inference
- Bias and confounding
- Effect modification
- Causal thinking
- Screening

**Best for:** Undergraduate and postgraduate students who need a structured introduction or refresher in epidemiological methods.

### 3. The Epidemiologist R Handbook

**Provider:** Applied Epi and contributing epidemiologists  
**Level:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Resource:** [Access the handbook](https://www.epirhandbook.com/en/)

A practical reference for conducting epidemiological and public health analyses using R.

**Includes:**
- Data cleaning and management
- Descriptive epidemiology
- Epidemiological tables and visualisation
- Outbreak investigation examples
- Reproducible R code
- Example datasets
- Offline-access options

**Best for:** Field epidemiologists, surveillance officers, public health researchers and analysts using R.

**Note:** This resource is also listed under R Programming because it is relevant to both categories.

### 4. Causal Inference: What If

**Authors:** Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Read the free textbook](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook)

A comprehensive textbook on estimating causal effects from observational and experimental data.

**Includes:**
- Counterfactuals and causal effects
- Randomised and observational studies
- Confounding and selection bias
- Causal diagrams
- Standardisation and inverse probability weighting
- The parametric g-formula
- Marginal structural models
- Time-varying treatments and confounders
- Downloadable datasets and supporting code

**Best for:** Epidemiologists, biostatisticians and postgraduate researchers interested in causal inference and advanced observational study methods.

### 5. Causal Inference: Practical R and Stata Code

**Book authors:** Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins  
**Code contributors:** Joy Shi, Sean McGrath, Eleanor Murray, Roger Logan and Tom Palmer  
**Level:** Intermediate to advanced  
**Resource:** [Access the practical code and exercises](https://remlapmot.github.io/cibookex-r/)

A practical companion resource containing reproducible examples associated with the textbook *Causal Inference: What If*.

**Includes:**
- R and Stata code
- Worked examples
- Causal inference methods
- Reproducible analyses
- Downloadable code materials

**Best for:** Researchers who want to implement causal inference methods using R or Stata.

**Note:** Use this resource alongside the original textbook for the methodological explanations.
